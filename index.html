How to make it harder

Earn the reps first

Choose a load that leaves about 2 good reps in reserve. Start at the lower end of the rep range and use controlled, comfortable motion.

Then add the smallest weight jump

When every work set reaches the top of its range with the same technique and effort, increase the load next time. Return toward the lower end of the range.

Progress range without forcing it

First improve control in the range you have. Gradually explore a little more comfortable motion. More discomfort is not a better stretch.

Adjust to your recovery

Weeks 4 and 8 are review points. If performance or recovery is falling, take a lighter week: about one-third fewer sets and keep 3-4 reps in reserve. A deload is a practical tool, not a mandatory calendar event.

What athletic means here

Move comfortably.
Stay capable.

Get stronger through useful ranges. Improve hip and shoulder motion, balance and basic conditioning. Train for daily life, not sprint testing.

Small changes, useful feedback.

Keep protein steady. Add rice, oats or fruit when you need more training fuel. If pursuing fat loss, use a modest deficit and watch both performance and the weight trend.

A reason behind
the routine.

The principles are research-informed. This exact schedule and your personal results have not been validated by a study.

What this plan cannot promise

A certain amount of muscle by a certain date. A guaranteed drop in body-fat percentage. A corrected pelvic tilt. A pain-free back from one routine.

Your starting point, consistency, food intake, sleep and symptoms matter. Use the checkpoints to adjust the plan, not to judge yourself against an invented transformation deadline.

Useful checkpoints

Weeks 1-2 · learn the baseline

Find comfortable loads and ranges. Log your starting measurements. Early scale changes can be water and glycogen.

Week 4 · look for a direction

Compare reps at the same load and movement comfort. Stretching studies show range can improve over weeks, but no specific cm gain is guaranteed.

Week 8 · compare consistently

Review waist, weekly weight averages, ankle measurements and lifting logs. Look for a sustained trend rather than one unusually good session.

Week 12 · choose the next focus

Keep what worked. Adjust food or training volume based on the data. Noticeable physique changes vary widely with training history and starting body composition.

If fat loss is your goal

A practical starting aim is about 0.25-0.75% of body weight per week, using the slower end when already lean or recovery is difficult. This coaching range is informed by slower-loss research, not directly tested as a complete range.

Maintaining strength during a cut is a useful outcome. A slower waist trend with stable weight can also be useful, but it does not prove muscle gain.

Back-aware, not back treatment.

Supported exercises reduce balance or trunk demands, but no exercise is universally pain-free. Choose a comfortable variation. Persistent or worsening symptoms deserve an individual assessment.

New weakness, numbness around the groin, or loss of bladder/bowel control requires urgent medical assessment.
