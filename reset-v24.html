Programming rule:

Fast Pace comes first for the next 13 days. After the race, the endurance plan shifts toward a VO₂-focused block: lots of easy running, one true VO₂ interval day, one threshold day, long easy aerobic work, strides/sprints, and progressive mileage. Harder is not automatically better.

Block 1 · Fast Pace

Restore rhythm, race-specific intervals, threshold, taper.

Block 2 · Reload

1–2 weeks to restore consistent mileage after the race.

Block 3 · VO₂ Development

6–10 weeks. Mostly easy volume + one VO₂max interval session (generally 2–4 min reps) + one threshold stimulus + long run.

Retest

Use repeatable race results and, when practical, a real lab VO₂ test. Treat watch VO₂ as a trend, not absolute truth.

APEX SCORECARD

What “most fit” means here.

Aerobic engine

VO₂ max trend toward 70, faster 5K/mile performances, more easy-volume tolerance.

Muscularity

Progressive strength and hypertrophy with emphasis on shoulders, arms, upper chest, back and legs.

Leanness / definition

Waist and appearance trend down while performance and muscle are preserved. No dehydration or starvation-based shortcuts.

Strength

Heavy force production, pull-up progression, unilateral strength and durable calves/ankles.

Speed + power

Acceleration, max velocity, jumps, reactive stiffness and change of direction after the race block.

Body control

Hanging strength, trunk control, carries, Copenhagen work, crawling, single-leg stability and landing control.

Consistency

Sleep, nutrition, work productivity and training adherence. The system has to survive real life.

WELL-BEING

Performance requires recovery.

Food

No crash cut before the race. High protein and enough carbohydrate around hard running.

After race

Use a modest deficit if waist loss is still the priority. Track weekly-average weight + waist.

Mobility

5–10 min comfortable hips, ankles, upper back and shoulders most days.

Sleep

Do not steal sleep to manufacture productivity.
